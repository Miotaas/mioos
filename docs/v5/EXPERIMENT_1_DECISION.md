# MioOS V5 — Experiment #1 Decision (v2)

Revised 2026-09-03 after scope correction. **Supersedes the newsletter-led v1**, archived as `EXPERIMENT_1_DECISION.v1-newsletter.md`.
**Awaiting approval. No code, no provisioning, no deployment.**

The question this document answers is no longer *"what media business earns fastest"* but
**"what is the smallest real experiment that begins proving MioOS can create, operate, monetise and eventually replicate profitable social-media properties?"**

---

## 0. What changed, and one warning

v1 optimised for time-to-revenue and landed on a newsletter. That answered the wrong question. Corrected here.

**But one part of the correction needs a guardrail.** The core-asset model — one research package fanning out to six platform-native assets — is the most seductive and most commonly failed idea in content operations. It usually produces six mediocre assets rather than one good one, because *platform-native is not adaptation, it is separate creative judgement*. YouTube's own inauthentic-content policy names content that "mimics existing formats to a degree that the videos feel interchangeable," and cross-posted vertical video is demonstrably down-ranked. *[Likely]*

**The distinction this experiment is built on:**

- **Shared and automated:** research, sourcing, claim provenance, data, generated diagrams, voice, assembly, captions, encoding, publishing prep, metrics ingestion.
- **Not shared, never templated:** the hook, the structure, the pacing, the reason each individual asset exists.

A Short is *not* a clip of the long-form. It is a separately conceived piece built from the same research. That rule is what keeps this out of the category that got 16 channels deleted in January 2026.

**Second correction to the correction:** five platforms in Experiment #1 tests nothing, because nothing can be attributed. Two distribution surfaces plus one owned layer.

## 1. Corrected Experiment #1 recommendation

### The property: a data-driven explainer channel on the physical economy

**Working thesis:** how the physical systems behind modern life actually work, and where they are breaking — ports, freight corridors, energy grids, data centres, water, chips — told through original analysis and purpose-built diagrams by someone who has worked inside these systems.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Property type** | YouTube-anchored multi-surface media property |
| **Canonical asset** | YouTube long-form, 10–14 minutes, one researched thesis per video |
| **Discovery surfaces** | YouTube Shorts (same channel, capped) + Instagram Reels |
| **Owned layer** | Email list from day one — capture destination, not the product |
| **Identity** | Brand-led channel, Mio named and audible. Not anonymous, not an AI persona. |
| **Visual language** | **Own-generated diagrams, maps and charts first.** Licensed/public-domain footage second, minimal. This is a budget constraint that happens to also be the differentiator and the copyright-risk control. |
| **Voice** | Mio's own voice, cloned. Exempt from YouTube AI-disclosure (own-voice cloning is explicitly listed as not requiring the label) and removes the human bottleneck from narration. |
| **Language** | English, global audience |

**Why YouTube long-form is the property and everything else is a surface:** it is the only one of the five platforms where a small audience monetises meaningfully, where content compounds through search rather than decaying in a feed, where sponsorship is straightforward at low subscriber counts, and where the archive becomes an asset. TikTok excludes the Netherlands from Creator Rewards; Instagram has no per-view revenue share; Facebook is invite-only. Those three are discovery, not property.

### The tie, and how it breaks

#1 and #2 tie. **Physical economy wins on two dimensions that matter more than the total:**

- **Replicability.** The format — original data, purpose-built diagrams, operator perspective — ports cleanly to a second vertical. "AI capability" is a single topic that eventually exhausts, and its authority is personal rather than systemic. Since the objective is *a portfolio of properties*, replicability is the deciding criterion.
- **Copyright and policy posture.** A diagram-first visual language means the channel owns almost every frame. The AI niche has a policy adjacency problem: YouTube's July 2026 clarification bars AI personas advising on finance and adjacent topics, and an AI-tools channel drifts toward that boundary constantly.

**Where your logistics knowledge lands:** ports, corridors, containers and supply chains are the visually strongest and most under-explained part of the physical economy. It is an execution advantage inside a larger niche, not a constraint on it — which is what you asked for.

### Why freight-only lost

Its defensibility score is the highest in the table and it still finishes 29 points behind, because **freight operations do not make good video.** Warehouses, TMS screens and rate sheets are visually inert; Shorts and Reels potential is near zero; the audience is small. It remains an excellent *newsletter* — which is exactly the trap v1 fell into: I scored the niche on the wrong medium.

## 3. Platform strategy

| Surface | Role | Cadence in Stage 1 | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **YouTube long-form** | **The property.** Canonical asset, search-compounding, sponsorable, archive value. | 8 videos / 12 weeks | The only surface where the asset appreciates |
| **YouTube Shorts** | Discovery into the same channel | **Capped at 2 per long-form** | The 274-channel study found Shorts cannibalise whole-channel RPM by up to 77% at 21+/month. Capping is not timidity — it is the finding. |
| **Instagram Reels** | Second discovery surface, different audience shape | 1 per long-form | Chosen over TikTok: TikTok's Content Posting API blocks public posting for unaudited apps, and NL is excluded from Creator Rewards. TikTok becomes a Stage-3 addition once the format is proven. |
| **Email list** | Owned layer, from day one | Weekly-ish | Your loop, correctly ordered: social discovery → owned audience → monetisation. The v1 newsletter finding survives — as a destination, not the product. |
| **Facebook** | **Not in Stage 1** | — | No evidence it earns its operational load for this audience. Revisit at Stage 3. |

**Deliberately two surfaces, not five.** With five you cannot tell which one produced a result, and a solo operator publishing to five surfaces publishes badly to all of them.

## 4. Content architecture — the core-asset model, done honestly

```
CORE RESEARCH PACKAGE          ← shared, automated, provenance-tracked
  thesis · sources · data · generated diagrams · fact table
        │
        ├── YouTube long-form (10–14 min)   ← canonical. Full argument.
        │
        ├── Short A  ← ONE startling number from the research.
        │              Separately conceived. Not a clip.
        ├── Short B  ← ONE counterintuitive mechanism.
        │              Different hook, different structure.
        │
        ├── Reel     ← Visual-first. The diagram IS the content.
        │              Different pacing, different CTA.
        │
        └── Email issue ← The written argument + the full source list.
                          The thing the long-form had to cut.
```

**What is genuinely shared:** the research, the sources, the numbers, the diagrams, the voice model, the encode presets.
**What is never shared:** the hook, the structure, the length logic, the CTA, the reason the asset exists.

Each derivative asset gets its own row in `content_item` with its own state, its own review and its own metrics — not a `derived_from` flag on one parent. That schema choice is what stops the pipeline sliding into templated output.

## 5. Minimum W3 required

W3 is **no longer deferred**. If social-media production is the hypothesis, the experiment must contain real production capability. But only the minimum:

| Capability | Build | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Voice** | ElevenLabs, Mio's cloned voice | Exempt from AI disclosure. Removes the narration bottleneck. |
| **Diagram / chart rendering** | **Programmatic, headless-browser or SVG → PNG** | The distinctive visual asset. Zero copyright risk. This is the single most important W3 component and the one that makes the channel look like nobody else. |
| **Assembly** | FFmpeg — timeline, transitions, audio normalisation | Deterministic. No AI. |
| **Captions** | whisper.cpp local, burnt-in | Free, no API |
| **Platform export presets** | 16:9 1080p · 9:16 1080×1920 | Deterministic encode profiles per surface |
| **Thumbnail** | AI stills + text composition | Cheap; iterate manually at first |
| **Explicitly NOT built** | AI video generation (Veo/Sora/Kling) | 90–97% of cost, and the exact aesthetic the enforcement waves targeted |
| **Manual in Stage 1** | Publishing, and any b-roll selection | Publishing stays human — required by YouTube's developer policies anyway |

**Consequence for infrastructure:** W3 in Stage 1 means the CX23 is no longer sufficient. See §8.

## 7. Monetisation architecture

Evaluate the **property**, not one payout. Diversification is the design goal — dependence on a single algorithm is the failure mode.

| Mechanism | Unlocks at | Realistic contribution | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct sponsorship** | ~5,000 subs | **Largest early line.** Niche B2B/industrial CPMs are strong and this audience attracts engineering, industrial-software and infrastructure advertisers | Does not require any platform programme |
| **Email sponsorship** | ~2,000 subs | $50–250/placement at B2B CPMs of $50–100+ *[Likely]* | The v1 finding, preserved and correctly positioned |
| **YouTube ads** | 1,000 subs + 4,000 hrs (**8,000 from 1 Feb 2027**) | Education/tech RPM band; meaningful only at scale | Long-form only. Shorts pool is a rounding error and pays nothing below 10M views/90d from Feb 2027 |
| **Affiliate** | Immediately | Modest in this niche — few natural products | Weakest line here; do not build strategy on it |
| **Digital product** | Month 12+ | Data products, briefings, or a paid research tier | Highest margin, latest |
| **TikTok / Instagram payouts** | — | **Assume €0** | NL excluded from Creator Rewards; IG has no rev share |

**Target mix at Stage 5:** roughly 50% sponsorship, 25% email sponsorship, 15% platform ads, 10% product. No single line above 60%.

## 8. Revised economics

W3 in Stage 1 changes both the server and the spend.

### Infrastructure

| | CX23 | **CX33** |
|---|---|---|
| Spec | 2 vCPU / 4 GB / 40 GB | **4 vCPU / 8 GB / 80 GB** |
| €/month | 6.04 | ~10.50 |
| Verdict | **No longer sufficient** — FFmpeg encoding plus headless diagram rendering plus Postgres/Redis/Node will contend and swap at 4 GB | **Recommended.** Encode headroom and disk for 8 long-form plus derivatives. |

**€10.50 + €2.10 backups ≈ €12.60/month.** Still not `aion-prod-1`. Disk growth is one-way — 80 GB is the deliberate starting point. Upgrade trigger stays a measurement: a render exceeding 15 minutes, or sustained swap.

| Captions | whisper.cpp local | €0 |
| Publishing | Manual | €0 |
| Contingency | | €36 |
| **Total** | | **€150** |

### The €150 ceiling holds — and the constraint is the strategy

A stock-footage subscription would add roughly €80 and push this to €230. **Do not buy it.** Forcing the visual language to own-generated diagrams, maps and charts is simultaneously: the cheapest option, the lowest copyright risk, the strongest differentiator against a genre that runs on recycled stock, and the furthest thing from the templated-stock-loop aesthetic that got channels purged in January 2026.

The budget constraint and the correct creative decision are the same decision. Keep the ceiling.

**Maximum risk at Stage 1: €150 and roughly 110 hours.** The hours are the real cost — 8 researched explainers at 10–12h each initially, falling as W1/W2 mature. That is ~9h/week and it is the number most likely to break this, not the money.

## 10. GO / PIVOT / KILL — Stage 1 in full

**GO** — all four must hold across 8 long-form videos:
- Median CTR **>4%**
- Median average view duration **>40%**
- Return-viewer rate **>10%**
- At least 2 videos above 3× channel median, and ≥150 subscribers

*Benchmarks are agency-sourced and directional, not authoritative. Re-baseline them against your own first 4 videos.*

**PIVOT** — signal present, execution wrong:
- CTR >4% but AVD <30% → packaging works, structure doesn't. Rewrite the format, keep the niche.
- AVD >45% but CTR <2% → content works, thumbnails/titles don't. Fixable, and the better problem to have.
- Long-form healthy, verticals flat → drop Reels, keep Shorts capped, add email weight.

**KILL** — CTR <2% **and** AVD <30% across all 8, or fewer than 60 subscribers at week 14. The niche does not want this treatment.

**Also kill, regardless of metrics:** cadence missed 4+ times in 12 weeks, or issue 04 proves unmakeable.

## 12. The strongest argument against this recommendation

**You are entering the single most competitive premium category on YouTube, and the white-space argument that justified freight does not apply here — it is the exact opposite.**

The data-driven explainer genre is occupied by Wendover, RealLifeLore, Johnny Harris, Practical Engineering, Half as Interesting and several hundred imitators. Several run six-figure production budgets, full-time researchers and motion designers. Viewers do not grade on a curve: your first video is compared to channels with five years of iteration, whether that is fair or not. The production-quality floor in this genre is brutally high and rising.

Against that, freight operations had a genuine moat — nobody is making it, you have real access, and there is no incumbent to be measured against.

**I traded a defensible small niche for a contested large one, and I should be explicit that this is a real trade, not a free upgrade.**

The counter-case, stated without overclaiming: the incumbents are generalist researchers reading public sources, and none of them has operated inside the systems they explain. Operator perspective is the one input they cannot buy. Whether audiences can perceive that difference in the first eight videos — before the channel has any reputation to trade on — is **genuinely unknown, and it is the main thing Stage 1 measures.**

If Stage 1 kills, the most likely reading is that the differentiation was real but invisible at this production quality. The correct response would then be to pivot *down* in audience size and *up* in specificity — back toward freight, where the moat exists and the bar is lower — rather than to keep spending against Wendover.

That fallback is deliberate: **v1 is archived in this folder, not deleted.**
